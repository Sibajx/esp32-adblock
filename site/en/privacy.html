Your privacy with SiBlock

SiBlock is made so nobody knows what you do on the internet, not even us. Here is everything that gets counted, what gets kept and what is never kept.

In short: apart from what your SiBlock always does to work (explained below), it sends nothing to SiBlock or anyone else unless you turn it on. The two options that send something are off by default.

The panel on the board is only in Spanish for now, so option names are shown in Spanish with their meaning next to them.

What SiBlock always does

To work, your SiBlock connects to the internet for three things, without sending anything of yours to SiBlock:

  • Look up the pages it doesn’t block. Just like your modem does, it asks Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Quad9 (9.9.9.9) for the address of every page that isn’t an ad. They see which pages are looked up from your home, just as your internet company would.
  • Download the ad list, once a day.
  • Ask for the time from pool.ntp.org or time.cloudflare.com, to check that downloads are safe.

If you turn on «Avisarme de versiones nuevas» (“Tell me about new versions”), it also connects once a day to siblock.s-dev.win to see if there is a new version, even when «Compartir datos anónimos» (“Share anonymous data”) is off.

If you turn on the extra lists (scams, ads in Spanish), it also downloads them once a day from GitHub and adblockplus.org. It sends them nothing of yours; like any website, they see your home’s internet address.

What this page counts

  • Visits. We use Cloudflare Web Analytics, with no cookies: it counts how many people open each page and from which country, without knowing who you are or following you to other pages.
  • Install steps. When you arrive, plug in the board, finish installing, enter your WiFi or open your panel, we add one to a counter for that day. No number of yours, no name and nothing stored in your browser goes with it: only the name of the step.
  • If your browser asks “do not track”, we count none of the above.
  • The page stores nothing in your browser and doesn’t ask you to create an account.

What the board sends (only if you turn it on)

In your panel, under «Compartir con SiBlock» (“Share with SiBlock”), there are two options. Both are off by default and you can turn them off whenever you want. When you erase the settings, they turn off by themselves.

«Compartir datos anónimos» (Share anonymous data)

If you turn it on, your SiBlock sends to siblock.s-dev.win once a day a random number for this install (it doesn’t say who you are), the SiBlock version and how many ads it removed that day. And, only when you send it, your modem brand, whether you could change the DNS and in which menu, or the site that shouldn’t have been blocked. It never sends your IP, the MAC, device names or which sites you visit. The server sees your country and your internet company from the connection, but doesn’t keep your IP. With this option off, SiBlock sends nothing to siblock.s-dev.win.

From each day’s check-in we keep:

  • a scrambled version of the install number (not the number itself);
  • the SiBlock version;
  • the country (the server sees it from the connection);
  • how many ads it removed that day;
  • the day (not the time).

Each day’s data is deleted after 400 days. The public statistics (in Spanish) only show it added up by country and by version.

«Avisarme de versiones nuevas» (Tell me about new versions)

If you turn it on, SiBlock checks once a day whether there is a new version. It only asks for a public file; it sends nothing of yours.

What is kept from reports

Reports (“which modem I have” and “this shouldn’t have been blocked”) are sent from your panel or from the reports page (in Spanish). They are options to pick; the only thing you type is the site name in “this shouldn’t have been blocked”, and it’s only accepted if it is a site name.

From “which modem I have” we keep:

  • the country and the internet company (the server sees them from the connection);
  • the modem brand, whether you could change the DNS and in which menu;
  • the day (not the time);
  • a scrambled version of the install number, to count only one report per home.

From “this shouldn’t have been blocked” we keep:

  • the site name and why (a page, an app, a game or other);
  • the day (not the time);
  • a scrambled version of the install number, to count only one report per home.

These reports are not deleted over time: they are the guide that helps other people with their modem.

We never keep your IP, the MAC of your devices, your name or your location. To stop abuse, the server looks at how many reports come from the same connection, but doesn’t keep that address.

The public statistics only show large groups: a country shows up with 5 boards or more, and a modem combination with 3 or more matching reports.

When you write to us

If you use the contact page (in Spanish), your email, your name (if you give it) and your message reach only SiBlock’s owner, to answer you. We don’t publish or share them, and they are deleted when no longer needed. You get an automatic reply that doesn’t repeat anything you wrote.

If you come from your panel, you can attach your SiBlock’s diagnosis (version, free memory, warnings and the last error when downloading the list). It’s only sent if you tick the box.

From messages we only count the topic (for example, “I can’t install”) to know what fails most.

So we don’t send more than one automatic reply a day to the same address, we keep a scrambled version of your email (not the email itself) only until the next day.

You can send nothing

SiBlock works just the same without sharing anything: leave both options off and don’t fill in the reports. If you have a question about your data, write to contacto@s-dev.win.
